  ## Science, Technology and Innovation
  *Alex reports a result and Sam asks what it does not prove.*

  **Alex**: The research asked whether the new tool shortens the task. The method was a timed test with two groups.

  **Sam**: What did it find?

  **Alex**: The group with the tool finished faster. That is the finding. The name of the tool is not itself the result.

  **Sam**: What does it not prove?

  **Alex**: It does not prove the tool works for every task, or that people will still use it next year.

  **Sam**: Then say the result, the method, and that limit together. A name alone is not a finding.

  **Sam**: How many people were in each group, and were the tasks the same?

  **Alex**: Thirty in each. The task was the same. Only the tool differed. That is why the faster finish can be tied to the tool.

  **Sam**: Did you ask them a month later whether they still used it?

  **Alex**: No. That is the limit I already named. The finding is about one timed test, not about next year.

  **Sam**: If a company uses the name of the tool as if it were the finding, what do you correct?

  **Alex**: I separate them. The name is a label. The finding is the faster finish in this test, with this limit.

  **Sam**: Would a third group, without any tool, have changed the method?

  **Alex**: It would strengthen it. We did not do that, so I will not claim we did. The result stands only as far as the two groups go.
