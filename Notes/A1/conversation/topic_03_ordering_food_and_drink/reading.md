  **Waiter**: Good evening. A table for two?

  **Mia**: Yes, please. By the window, if that is possible.

  **Ben**: I would like the pasta, please, and a glass of water.

  **Mia**: I will have the grilled fish, and a lemonade, please.

  **Waiter**: Of course. I will bring the drinks now.

  **Mia**: That was delicious. Can we have the bill, please?

  **Waiter**: Of course. Here it is. Have a lovely evening.

  **Ben**: One moment, please. Before we pay, may we add a dessert?

  **Mia**: The cake for me, please. Ben, do you want fruit?

  **Waiter**: I will bring them. The fish and the pasta are already on the bill.

  ## Coffee Culture Around the World
  *A short story about a traveler's coffee stops in three cities.*

  Anna loves coffee, and she notices something new every time she travels. Last spring, she visited three cities in two weeks, and each one taught her something different about ordering a simple cup of coffee.

  Her first stop was Rome. She walked into a small café near her hotel, and everyone was standing at the counter, not sitting at tables. She ordered an espresso, drank it in three sips, paid, and left - the whole thing took less than five minutes. "Nobody sits down here," she thought, "everyone's in a hurry, but somehow it doesn't feel rushed."

  A few days later, she landed in New York. This time, she ordered a large coffee "to go" from a busy shop near Times Square, and she drank it while walking to meet a friend. Nobody around her was sitting either, but for a different reason - they were all walking somewhere, coffee in hand, coffee cups everywhere on the street.

  Her last stop was London. There, a friend invited her to a small café for "a proper cup of tea," and Anna almost forgot she wanted coffee at all. They sat by the window for over an hour, ordered a pot of tea and a coffee, and just talked. Nobody rushed them, and the waiter never brought the bill until they asked for it.

  By the end of her trip, Anna realized the coffee itself didn't change much from city to city - but the way people ordered it, and the time they gave themselves to drink it, told her something about each place. And in every café, the same simple words worked everywhere: I would like…, Can I have…, please, and to go, or for here.
