  ## Ability, Obligation, and Want
  *Sam asks what Alex can do, must do, and wants to do this week.*

  **Sam**: Can you come to the meeting on Wednesday?

  **Alex**: I can come, but I must leave at four. I want to catch the early bus.

  **Sam**: You may leave at four. You do not have to stay for the last point.

  **Alex**: Good. I want to help, but I cannot stay late.

  **Sam**: Then you should speak first. After that, you can go.

  **Alex**: I will do that. I can send the notes after I am on the bus.

  **Sam**: Can you send the notes before five?

  **Alex**: I can, if I leave at four. I must be on the bus by ten past four.

  **Sam**: You do not have to write every word. You should write the decision and the date.

  **Alex**: I want to do that on the bus. May I leave the room at four exactly?

  **Sam**: Yes, you may. You must not miss the first point, because you should speak first.

  **Alex**: I can speak at the start, and I cannot stay for the last point. I do not have to.

  **Sam**: Then that is the plan. You should be clear, and you can go when you must.

  **Alex**: I will. I want to help, I can help at the start, and I must catch the bus.

  **Alex**: 最初に話せます。最後の点には残れません。残る義務はありません。

  **Sam**: ではそれが計画です。明確であるべきで、行かなければならないときに行けます。

  **Alex**: そうします。助けたいです。最初には助けられます。バスには乗らなければなりません。

  ## Language tips

  - modal + base verb: She can swim (no -s)
  - can = ability/permission
  - must / have to = obligation
  - want to = plan/desire
  - mustn't (forbidden) ≠ don't have to (not necessary)

  - darf nicht (verboten) ≠ muss nicht (nicht notwendig)
