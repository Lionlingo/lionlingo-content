## explanation

en:
  Literary criticism requires precise aesthetic vocabulary. A metaphor is a figure of speech comparing two things without like or as; a motif is a recurring thematic element; narrative voice is who tells the story and from what perspective; aesthetic refers to beauty, form, and artistic effect. C2 learners discuss poetry and prose at seminar level - not plot summary.

  Key terms: stream of consciousness; unreliable narrator; imagery; subtext; intertextuality. Verbs of analysis: point toward, underline, stage, reflect, problematise. Structure a critique: thesis → textual evidence → interpretation → context. Quote briefly; integrate citations with the author claims or the speaker formulates.

C2 diskutiert Lyrik und Prosa auf Seminarniveau - keine Inhaltsangabe.

## rules

- metaphor = comparison without like or as
- motif = recurring thematic element
- narrative voice = who tells the story and how
- aesthetic = artistic beauty, form, and effect
- Leitmotiv = guiding recurring motif
- unreliable narrator = account reader cannot fully trust
- Thesis → evidence → interpretation → context
- Avoid plot summary - analyse how, not only what
