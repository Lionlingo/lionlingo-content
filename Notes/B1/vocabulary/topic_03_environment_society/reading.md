  ## Environment, Recycling and Society
  *Alex and Sam sort the building's waste and tell the neighbors.*

  **Sam**: The bins are full again. We throw away too much.

  **Alex**: Let us reduce first, reuse the boxes, and recycle only what remains.

  **Sam**: Paper in the blue bin, glass in the green one. Is that right?

  **Alex**: Yes. If we separate them, the city can use the material again.

  **Sam**: I will put a short note by the door for the neighbors.

  **Alex**: Good. This small habit is our part of a larger problem.

  **Sam**: What about plastic? The note by the door does not name it.

  **Alex**: Plastic goes in the yellow bin, if it is clean. Food on it means it cannot be recycled.

  **Sam**: So we reduce first. We wash the box and use it again before we recycle it.

  **Alex**: Yes. A neighbor asked me yesterday why the blue bin was mixed. I said paper only.

  **Sam**: I will add that to the note: paper, glass, plastic, and what we refuse to throw away.

  **Alex**: Also say the collection day. The city takes the yellow bin on Wednesday.

  **Sam**: If one neighbor does not follow it, do we correct the person or the bin?

  **Alex**: Fix the bin, and leave the note. A habit changes when the instruction is clear, not when we shame a person.
