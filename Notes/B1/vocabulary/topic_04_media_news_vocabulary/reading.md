  ## Media, News and Current Events
  *Alex and Sam read the same headline and check the source.*

  **Sam**: The headline says the bridge is closed. Is that the whole report?

  **Alex**: No. The report says it is closed until six, according to the city office.

  **Sam**: Who is the source?

  **Alex**: The city office, named in the second line. The headline left the time out.

  **Sam**: So the current event is a closure until six, not a permanent one.

  **Alex**: Yes. I will read the report, not only the headline, before I change my route.

  **Sam**: Is the city office the source, or is the newspaper the source?

  **Alex**: The city office is the source of the fact. The newspaper is the outlet that reported it.

  **Sam**: The headline says closed. The report says until six. Which line should I trust for my route?

  **Alex**: Trust the report. A headline is a short claim. It can leave out the time and still be printed.

  **Sam**: I will change my route only until six. After six I can use the bridge again.

  **Alex**: That matches the report. If a later update removes the six o'clock, the event has changed.

  **Sam**: I will check the outlet once more at five, not only the headline on my phone.

  **Alex**: Good. Source, outlet, time, and the limit of the closure. That is the story you need.
